**Revised Prompt:**

You will be presented with logical arguments. Your task is to determine whether each argument is **valid** or **invalid** based solely on its logical structure, completely ignoring the factual truth or falsity of the premises. Additionally, when an argument is **invalid**, you must identify the **specific formal fallacy** by name.

**Critical Instructions:**
- **Validity** depends exclusively on whether the conclusion necessarily follows from the premises based on the argument's logical form
- **Soundness** (which requires both validity and true premises) is NOT being evaluated
- You MUST NOT consider whether premises are actually true or false in reality
- Focus ONLY on the structural relationship between premises and conclusion
- **CRITICAL SEPARATION OF FORM AND CONTENT:** You must completely disregard the specific content, emotional tone, or apparent truthfulness of the premises. Even if premises are obviously false, emotionally charged, or counterintuitive, your evaluation must remain strictly structural.

**Systematic Evaluation Process:**

1. **Extract Logical Structure:** Convert the argument into symbolic form using propositional variables (P, Q, R, etc.), stripping away all semantic content

2. **Compare to Known Patterns:** Match the symbolic structure against:
   - Valid forms: modus ponens, modus tollens, disjunctive syllogism, hypothetical syllogism
   - Invalid forms (with specific fallacy names): affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent, fallacy of the undistributed middle, illicit major/minor, false dilemma, equivocation

3. **Verify Structural Relationships:** For each comparison:
   - **CRITICAL STRUCTURAL CLUE FOR CONDITIONALS:** Pay special attention to what the second premise affirms or denies:
     - **Modus Ponens (VALID):** Second premise affirms the **antecedent** (P in "If P then Q")
     - **Affirming the Consequent (INVALID):** Second premise affirms the **consequent** (Q in "If P then Q")
     - **Modus Tollens (VALID):** Second premise denies the **consequent** (not Q in "If P then Q")  
     - **Denying the Antecedent (INVALID):** Second premise denies the **antecedent** (not P in "If P then Q")
   - **KEY DISTINCTION:** The validity depends entirely on whether the reasoning flows from antecedent to consequent (valid) or consequent to antecedent (invalid)
   - **WHY THIS MATTERS:** Focusing on whether the second premise affirms/denies the antecedent or consequent prevents confusion with the argument's content because it isolates the pure logical structure, making it impossible to be misled by emotionally compelling or seemingly true premises

4. **Make Determination with Explicit Reasoning:** 
   - If the structure guarantees the conclusion follows necessarily → **valid**
   - If the structure does not guarantee the conclusion follows necessarily → **invalid** AND specify the **exact formal fallacy name**

**Critical Structural Discrimination:**
- **Avoid Pattern Matching Errors:** Arguments that appear superficially similar may have fundamentally different logical structures. You must:
  - **Identify the core logical operators** (if-then, and, or, not) rather than relying on surface wording
  - **Check propositional scope and distribution** to distinguish between valid syllogisms and fallacies like undistributed middle
  - **Verify operator precedence** when multiple logical operators are present
