Read the question carefully. Before finalizing your answer, reason step-by-step by first identifying the specific geometric principle or transformation type governing the pattern—such as rotation, reflection, translation, congruence, similarity, or symmetry. Then, apply verification steps by analyzing the geometric shapes and explicitly checking for consistency with fundamental geometric principles. Compare the relative positions and orientations of geometric elements in each option to determine which one logically completes the pattern.  

**Crucial step:** When verifying geometric properties, explicitly distinguish between visual appearance and mathematically rigorous definitions. For example, confirm congruence by checking exact side lengths and angle measures, not just shape resemblance; verify symmetry by testing for precise reflective or rotational properties, not approximate looks. This prevents common errors such as misidentifying similar shapes as congruent or assuming symmetry where none exists mathematically.  

**Additional guidance for transformations:** When analyzing transformations, carefully distinguish between rotation and reflection, especially when shapes appear visually similar. For rotations, track the consistent angular change and center of rotation; for reflections, identify the line of symmetry and verify that corresponding points are equidistant from it. If the transformation is ambiguous, test each possibility against the sequence’s pattern and eliminate options that violate geometric consistency.  

**Explicit instruction for distinguishing visual estimation from mathematical verification in transformations:**  
- **For rotations:** Do not rely solely on visual orientation; mathematically verify by calculating the angle and direction of rotation and confirming that all points move consistently around a fixed center without altering distances or angles.  
- **For reflections:** Avoid assuming symmetry based on appearance; rigorously test for a reflection line by ensuring that each point and its image are equidistant from the line and that the line is perpendicular to the segment connecting them.  
- **General rule:** If visual inspection suggests a transformation, cross-check it with geometric axioms—e.g., rotations preserve orientation, while reflections reverse it; use coordinate or measurement-based checks to resolve ambiguity.
